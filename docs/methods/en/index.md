<section class="field-index__section" id="project-based-learning">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Project-based learning</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Learn by delivering a complete brief under constraints — not by collecting named techniques in isolation. The project is the unit of meaning; techniques serve the brief.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Receive or co-frame a design brief with criteria and deadline.</li><li>Plan milestones (research, diverge, select, develop, deliver).</li><li>Submit artefact + process note that explains decisions.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>

<section class="field-index__section" id="problem-solving-studio">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Problem-solving studio</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Treat creativity as situated problem work — frame the issue, explore options, test fitness, and defend the choice. Wicked briefs need framing before tools.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Name the problem and what would count as a good-enough answer.</li><li>Generate options without early judgment.</li><li>Select with explicit criteria and test against the brief.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>

<section class="field-index__section" id="diverge-converge-workshop">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Diverge–converge workshop</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Time-boxed sessions that separate opening (many options) from closing (selection). Fluency without flexibility is one idea in many costumes.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Protect a diverge window (quantity + variety).</li><li>Switch modes deliberately into converge (criteria + pick).</li><li>Record what was discarded and why.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>

<section class="field-index__section" id="critique-cycle">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Critique cycle</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Peer and tutor critique as a learning method — give/receive feedback that improves the next iteration, not vanity praise. Process over polish alone.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Present the brief, constraints, and current state briefly.</li><li>Ask for specific feedback (criteria, gaps, next test).</li><li>Log one change you will make before the next checkpoint.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>

<section class="field-index__section" id="iterative-prototyping">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Iterative prototyping</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Make rough, early versions so reaction teaches faster than speculation. Defer final polish until the idea has survived contact with users or peers.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Prototype the riskiest assumption first.</li><li>Show it to someone who can react honestly.</li><li>Revise once; decide when the brief is answered.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>

<section class="field-index__section" id="process-portfolio">
<article class="field-card field-card--method">
<h2 class="field-card__title">Process portfolio</h2>
<p class="field-card__summary">Evidence of learning is the trail — sketches, discarded options, criteria, and reflections — not only the final image. Authorship includes how meaning was put into circulation and revised.
</p>
<ol class="field-card__steps"><li>Keep a dated process folder for every project.</li><li>Include at least one discarded path with a one-line reason.</li><li>Disclose tools (including AI) and what you still own as judgment.</li></ol>
</article>
</section>
