1. In the study by Dow and colleagues, novice designers made several prototypes in parallel before critique, compared with critique after each single prototype. What did the parallel group do?

  1. They produced fewer but more polished designs.
  2. They performed the same as the other group.
  3. They lost confidence because of the extra work.
  4. They produced better-performing and more varied designs, and reported a larger gain in confidence at this kind of task.
Show answer

Answer: (D) They produced better-performing and more varied designs, and reported a larger gain in confidence at this kind of task.

Check it in the lesson: Lab Exercise 1, Source line · (Dow et al. 2010)

2. Student A makes one prototype, gets feedback, revises, and repeats three times. Student B makes three different prototypes and gets feedback on all three at once. Which one is parallel prototyping, and what did the study find?

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: Student B. In the study, the parallel group produced better-performing and more varied designs (and reported a larger gain in confidence) than the group that got critique after each single prototype.

Check it in the lesson: Lab Exercise 1, Source line · (Dow et al. 2010)

4. Why does U3 Exercise 1 start the same problem from three different entry points?

  1. Because a different entry point will usually mean a different train of ideas.
  2. To split the work among teammates.
  3. To save time on the problem.
  4. Because the brief asks for three products.

Answer: (A) Because a different entry point will usually mean a different train of ideas.

5. Your revised version passes its test question, but you can see one more improvement. Following idea 3, do you keep revising? Justify.

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: No — exit when the test passes, even if another improvement is possible; continuing risks fixation, refining one option without considering others.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 3 and Lab Exercise 2 step 7 · (Dow et al. 2010)
