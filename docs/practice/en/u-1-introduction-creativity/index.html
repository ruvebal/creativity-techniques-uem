1. In Csikszentmihalyi's system of three parts, what is the "field"?

  1. A set of rules and symbols, such as the notation a graphic designer has to learn.
  2. The person who brings a change the field has not yet accepted.
  3. The material or the file that holds the signs of the finished work.
  4. The people who decide what gets in, such as teachers, clients, curators and critics.
Show answer

Answer: (D) The people who decide what gets in, such as teachers, clients, curators and critics.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass, "A myth to drop" · (Csikszentmihalyi 1996)

2. For a café menu you try eight type treatments without judging any of them, then keep the two a waiter can read at arm's length. Which move is opening and which is closing?

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: Trying eight treatments without judging is opening (divergent thinking); keeping the two that can be read at arm's length is closing (convergent thinking, selecting what fits the brief).

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 1 (Open, then close) · (Craft 2000)

3. Which of the four abilities is the ability to produce a large number of ideas or solutions to a problem rapidly?

  1. Fluency.
  2. Flexibility.
  3. Originality.
  4. Elaboration.
Show answer

Answer: (A) Fluency.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 2 (Four skills that tests measure) · (Chen 2011)

4. A student listed the most uses in the alternative-uses Lab and concludes that they are the most talented designer in the class. Evaluate this conclusion.

Answer: It does not follow. A score like this may say little about talent in a specific field; the lesson asks you to treat the numbers as practice, not a verdict, and a long list does not show which idea serves a real brief.

5. Buchanan, following Rittel, says something about every formulation of a wicked problem. What?

  1. It has one definitive formulation that every experienced designer in the field would write the same way.
  2. It corresponds to the formulation of a solution — how you state the problem already points to an answer.
  3. It can be solved by testing alone, with no need to state the problem again.
  4. It should be left untouched until a finished solution already exists.
Show answer

Answer: (B) It corresponds to the formulation of a solution — how you state the problem already points to an answer.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 4 (Name the unclear problem first) · (Buchanan 1992)
