2. For a packaging brief you have twenty bottle shapes. Describe their fluency and flexibility, and say how you could add kinds.

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Answer: High fluency but only one kind (low flexibility). Add different kinds of idea, for example a refill pouch, a label-free bottle or a deposit scheme.

3. What "fixation" effect does Cross report, following Jansson and Smith?

  1. Showing people an example solution makes their own designs copy its features.
  2. Designers drop their first concept as soon as problems appear.
  3. Seeing an example always makes designs more original.
  4. Fixation affects only beginners.
Show answer

Answer: (A) Showing people an example solution makes their own designs copy its features.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 3 (Get past the obvious) · (Cross 2006)

4. What did one line of studies find about "what if?" (counterfactual) thinking?

  1. It always improved the generation of new ideas.
  2. It helped people link given ideas but hurt the generation of new ones.
  3. It had no effect on creative tasks.
  4. It hurt linking ideas but helped generating new ones.
Show answer

Answer: (B) It helped people link given ideas but hurt the generation of new ones.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 4 (Role and constraint tools) · (Wong, Galinsky, and Kray 2009)

5. After a workshop your wall holds forty sticky notes, and a teammate says the workshop clearly produced better ideas. Evaluate the claim.

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: Not shown. A workshop produces material you did not have before; whether it is better must be judged against the brief. Workshops target the dimensions most creativity tests measure (fluency, flexibility, originality), which is not the same as better ideas.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 6 (Workshops are means, not genius) · (Csikszentmihalyi 1996)
