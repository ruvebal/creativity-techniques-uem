1. In the small laboratory study cited in idea 1, which kind of meditation helped people on a divergent-thinking task?

  1. Focused-attention meditation, which keeps attention fixed on one chosen object.
  2. Walking meditation practised outdoors for the whole of the session.
  3. No meditation at all, only a rest of the same length before the task.
  4. Open-monitoring meditation (noticing whatever comes, without fixing on one thing).
Show answer

Answer: (D) Open-monitoring meditation (noticing whatever comes, without fixing on one thing).

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 1 (Generate, then select) · (Colzato, Ozturk, and Hommel 2012)

2. For a night-bus poster you have fifteen headlines, all puns on the route name. Describe their fluency and flexibility, and say how you could add kinds.

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: High fluency but only one kind (low flexibility). Add different kinds of idea, for example a photograph, a diagram, or a question addressed to the rider.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 2 (Fluency with flexibility) · (Csikszentmihalyi 1996)

3. What "fixation" effect does Cross report, following Jansson and Smith?

  1. Showing people an example solution makes their own designs copy its features.
  2. Designers drop their first concept as soon as problems appear in it.
  3. Seeing an example always makes the next designs more original than the example.
  4. Fixation of this kind affects only people who are just beginning.
Show answer

Answer: (A) Showing people an example solution makes their own designs copy its features.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 3 (Get past the obvious) · (Cross 2006)

4. What did one line of studies find about "what if?" (counterfactual) thinking?

  1. It always improved the generation of brand-new ideas from a blank page.
  2. It helped people link given ideas but hurt the generation of new ones.
  3. It had no measurable effect on either linking ideas or generating new ones.
  4. It hurt the linking of given ideas but helped the generation of new ones.
Show answer

Answer: (B) It helped people link given ideas but hurt the generation of new ones.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 4 (Role and constraint tools) · (Wong, Galinsky, and Kray 2009)

5. After a packaging workshop a teammate points at eighteen sketches and says the session clearly produced better ideas because there are so many. Evaluate the claim.

Apply the lesson to this situation in two or three sentences, then compare.

Show answer

Answer: Not shown. A workshop produces material you did not have before; whether it is better must be judged against the brief. The dimensions most workshops try to raise (fluency, flexibility, originality) are not the same thing as better ideas.

Check it in the lesson: Masterclass idea 6 (Workshops are means, not genius) · (Csikszentmihalyi 1996)
