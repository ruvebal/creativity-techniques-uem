## 🎯 Learning objectives

By the end of this unit, you should be able to:

1. Treat **creative confidence** as something you practice — not a feeling you wait for.
2. Name how **beliefs** (“Can I?” and “Should I?”) turn potential into action under a brief.
3. Use **success experiences** to decide what to repeat, revise, or drop.
4. **Name blocks** and restart with constraints, peers, or prompts — without shame.
5. Plan **habits** that survive after the workshop energy fades.
6. Exercise **autonomy with accountability** — own your process inside the brief and among peers.

## Masterclass

*Session block 2 · six ideas.*

### 1 · Confidence is practiced

**Creative confidence grows from doing creative work — not from waiting to feel gifted.**

U1 argued creativity can be taught; U6 asks you to **rehearse** that claim in your own folder. Small finished attempts beat big intentions. Talent myths make blocks feel permanent; practice makes them workable.

**In practice:** before a crit, list three concrete moves you tried this week — not three adjectives about your mood.

**Try it:** [Reflection-in-action log]({{ '/exercises/en/reflection-in-action-log/' | relative_url }}) logs surprises **while** you work.

### 2 · Beliefs convert potential

**Believing you can and should act creatively turns ability into action.**

Two gates appear often: *Can I do this creatively here?* and *Should I spend creative effort on this problem?* Both must open for agency. A brief gives you permission to act — it is not the enemy of creativity.

**In practice:** when you stall on a team project, write which gate is closed; ask one peer to name evidence you already have.

**Try it:** [Mental locks audit]({{ '/exercises/en/mental-locks-audit/' | relative_url }}) names the belief that closed an idea.

### 3 · Mastery feeds regulation

**Success experiences teach what to repeat, revise, or drop.**

“Mastery” here means **useful feedback from your own work**, not perfection. When something works once, decide whether to repeat it on purpose; when it fails, decide whether to revise or drop without drama.

**In practice:** after a good sketch, write one sentence: *I will repeat this because…*

**Try it:** [Reflection-in-action log]({{ '/exercises/en/reflection-in-action-log/' | relative_url }}) links moves into a short account.

### 4 · Blocks are part of the path

**Name blocks; use constraints, peers, and prompts to restart.**

Blocks are normal — techniques from U2–U5 are restart tools. Hiding a block wastes the chance to learn from it. Peers are not judges of your character; they are readers of your work.

**In practice:** when stuck, pick one constraint (timebox, material limit, oblique prompt) before you declare the idea dead.

**Try it:** [Mental locks audit]({{ '/exercises/en/mental-locks-audit/' | relative_url }}) revisits a dropped idea for five minutes.

### 5 · Sustain beyond the workshop

**Personal development means habits under real briefs — not a one-day high.**

Workshop energy fades. Portfolios accumulate if you tie practice to **deliverables you already owe**. The course ends; the habit can remain if it is anchored to making.

**In practice:** schedule one 10-minute log after each Lab session for the rest of the semester.

**Try it:** the [Workshop](#workshop) paragraph draft becomes D1 defence evidence.

### 6 · Autonomy with accountability

**Own your process inside the brief — without romantic isolation.**

Autonomy is self-direction **within** constraints: brief, deadline, peers, publics. Accountability includes declaring assistance, citing influences, and showing process — same rules as every unit.

**In practice:** share one unfinished version with a peer before you polish — ask what they need to see to trust your next step.

**Try it:** both Lab exercises produce traces you can show without oversharing private notes.

## Workshop {#workshop}

From session 4: half **D2 Transposition**, half **D3 Atrium**.

**Task:** draft a **six-sentence D1 confidence paragraph**: (1) a block you named, (2) the restart move, (3) one success, (4) one revision, (5) one peer moment, (6) one open question for your defence.

**Done enough:** paragraph saved and one habit named that you will repeat before the defence.

---

## Conclusion

Personal development in this course means **repeatable studio behaviour under briefs** — logs, restarts, and evidence you can show in a defence. Which single habit from U6 will still be true the week after the final Atrium event?
