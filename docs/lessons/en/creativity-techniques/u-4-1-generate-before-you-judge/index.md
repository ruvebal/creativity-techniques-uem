## 🎯 Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

1. Read a workplace brief and name its user, constraint, and open question.
2. Use an unrelated image to make several possible connections to the brief.
3. Build on a teammate’s connection without judging the whole idea set too early.
4. Save a trace that separates generated ideas from ideas chosen later.
5. Explain why a classroom prompt does not prove what a real visitor needs.

## Analysis

*Session block 1 · Analysis opens the class.*

From session 3, two students defend a D1 piece for about 15 minutes each. Start with **language** (the sign system or visual code), **medium** (the form that carries it, such as print or a screen), and **support** (the material or platform that holds it). Then ask who made the piece, who will meet it, and what changes when it moves from a studio draft to a public sign. A selection decision can travel into a pitch deck, but each change of support brings a new audience and a new chance to misread the idea.

**Debate prompt:** who gets time to try an unfinished idea when a deadline is close, and whose contribution stays visible in the final pitch? This is the unit’s labour question: creative work needs credit as well as a clever prompt.

## Masterclass

*Session block 2 · Ideas for carrying generation into work. Each one gives a claim, the evidence for it, a design example, and the Lab exercise where you try it.*

### 1 · A deadline does not have to judge every minute

**A team can keep its deadline and still protect a short time to generate.** Ask a Crab begins with a workplace problem, brings in pictures unrelated to it, and lets participants write and build on connections before collecting and evaluating them [(Michalko 2010, 440)](#ref-michalko-2010). In a design team, that means setting a brief and a timer, then postponing the “best idea” conversation until the first round is on the table. The class timing below is a professor proposal; it is not a time prescribed by the source. What can your team stop polishing for twenty minutes so the idea stage gets a real place?

**Try it:** [Lab · Ask a Crab](#lab-exercise-1) — keep the generation trace before making a selection.

### 2 · An unrelated image can change the route

**A visual prompt is useful when it gives the brief a new angle.** The source blueprint asks teams to look at pictures unrelated to the problem, write associations, share them, and develop new connections before evaluation [(Michalko 2010, 440)](#ref-michalko-2010). For an arrival sign, a picture of a river delta might suggest branching routes; it does not tell us whether a visitor can read them. The image opens a possibility, while a later check must ask whether that possibility serves the brief. Which connection would you keep long enough to sketch before deciding whether it fits?

**Try it:** [Lab · Ask a Crab](#lab-exercise-1) — record the picture and the path from it to the idea.

### 3 · Save the path, not just the favourite

**An idea list is a record of thinking, not a verdict on the people who made it.** Read each association back, add a modification where it helps, and note who contributed the change. The final portfolio trace should show the prompt, the first connections, and the changes made in conversation. That makes later selection easier to inspect and keeps group labour visible. If a polished concept hides the route that produced it, what would a teammate need to recover?

**Try it:** [Lab · Ask a Crab](#lab-exercise-1) — save a trace that another person can follow.

## Lab (Portfolio)

*Session block 3 · Make a trace for your portfolio index. This is the first half of a two-lesson exercise chain; U4.2 continues from the saved idea set.*

### Lab · Ask a Crab — picture diverge {#lab-exercise-1}

- **Practises:** generation from visual prompts; building on a teammate’s connection; delaying selection.
- **Time:** about 20 minutes proposed for this class adaptation; your professor sets the actual class clock.
- **Group:** teams of 3–5.
- **Materials:** one shared design brief; unrelated pictures; sticky notes or a shared board; a timer.
- **Steps:**
  1. Read the brief aloud and name the user, the constraint, and the question the team needs to solve.
  2. Give each person two or three unrelated pictures. Each person writes a possible connection to the brief for each picture.
  3. Take turns reading connections aloud. Teammates may add a modification or a new route; record additions beside the first idea.
  4. Stop before ranking. Mark the ideas that need a later selection conversation and carry the full set into U4.2.
- **Portfolio trace:** one-sentence brief; picture titles or images; initial connections; later additions; note that selection was deferred.
- **Example trace:** *Illustrative example (not student work)* — brief: “Help first-time visitors locate the accessible Atrium entrance.” Picture: river delta. Initial connection: show more than one approach route. Addition: use a clear landmark before the fork. Selection: deferred to U4.2.
- **Judged by:** whether the trace shows a clear link from brief to picture to idea, whether additions are recorded, and whether the team keeps generation separate from selection.
- **Source:** Ask a Crab’s five-step blueprint; this card adapts its first four steps to a classroom simulation [(Michalko 2010, 440)](#ref-michalko-2010).

## Workshop

From session 4, Workshop time is split about half for D2 Transposition and half for the D3 Atrium final event. D1 analysis never receives Workshop time. For this lesson, translate one generated idea into a quick representation and label its language, medium, and support; note what changes if it moves from the portfolio board to an Atrium sign.

## Conclusion

The river picture can open a route, but it cannot tell you whether a real visitor will find the entrance. The lesson leaves that question for a field check, an accessibility review, or a conversation with the people who use the space. Which assumption in your idea list would you test first? The next step is not another idea list; it is finding out what the sketch still cannot know.
