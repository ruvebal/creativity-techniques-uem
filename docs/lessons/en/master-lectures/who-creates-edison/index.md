> **A famous name is not the same as a contributor, an owner, or a company.**

## Learning objectives

By the end you can:

1. Tell apart **invention**, **improvement**, **sale**, **credit**, and **taking without credit** — without reducing the story to “genius” or “theft.”
2. Read a product on three levels: **object**, **system**, **money and power**.
3. Look for five kinds of evidence: technique, labour, law, finance, public story.
4. Draw two separate maps: **who made it** and **who gets the value**.
5. Redesign ownership — and say who pays to build, keep, and improve it.

## Series question

> How does shared work become a product, a property right, an industry — and a story about one genius?

Creativity research often starts with the person. A systems view asks how a field *recognises* work [(Csikszentmihalyi 1996, chap. 3)](#ref-csikszentmihalyi-1996). We stay with products so you can test that difference.

Keep the course triad on every case: **language** (claims, patents, brands) · **medium** (how it reaches people) · **support** (hardware, wiring, film, servers).

### The public story is made, too

Early “Wizard of Menlo Park” lives left out hard family facts [(Robb 2024, 242)](#ref-robb-2024). Mina Edison worked the press and “helped cultivate Edison’s public image as a genius” [(Robb 2024, 243)](#ref-robb-2024). The name itself became something to sell: the sons traded on it; Tom Jr. marketed an “Edison Junior Improved” lamp that was basically his father’s invention [(Robb 2024, 243–44)](#ref-robb-2024).

This source is a **book review** — a lead, not a final verdict. The reviewer also rejects feelings attributed to Mina with no quote or cite [(Robb 2024, 244)](#ref-robb-2024). Do the same: no claim without evidence.

## Five cases (group work)

For your case, name: the contribution · who is often left out · the enabling system · what the evidence **cannot** prove. Explain — do not accuse.

### 1 · Electric light

Others worked on lamps. The laboratory’s strength was a **working system**: durable lamps plus generators, wiring, meters, sockets, and a company. A patent fight is not a court naming one inventor of “the light bulb.”

> When is building the whole system itself the creative act?

<figure class="lesson-figure" id="figure-masterclass-3">
<img src="{{ '/assets/images/deck-media/480daebe54e4a0ce.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Patent drawing of an electrical distribution network: conductors running around lettered blocks from a central connection point, feeder lines to dial indicators, and regulating coils, signed by two witnesses and, for the inventor, by his attorneys." loading="lazy" decoding="async">
<figcaption>{% include lesson-figure.html deck="who-creates-edison" slide="masterclass-3" %}</figcaption>
</figure>

A patent can claim a network, not only a bulb. Who else’s labour did this system need?

### 2 · The phonograph

A **strong** Edison contribution — and still shared work (idea, machinist, earlier recording ideas). Correcting the lone-genius myth does not erase real invention.

> How should credit differ for the idea, the first build, and making it reproducible?

<figure class="lesson-figure" id="figure-masterclass-2">
<img src="{{ '/assets/images/deck-media/82e0df15dbcda80f.webp' | relative_url }}" alt="Notebook page with two pencil sketches of possible phonograph designs, dated 29 November 1877 and signed by Edison, Charles Batchelor, and John Kruesi; the lower design is the one the National Park Service says was assigned to Kruesi to build." loading="lazy" decoding="async">
<figcaption>{% include lesson-figure.html deck="who-creates-edison" slide="masterclass-2" %}</figcaption>
</figure>

Two designs, three signatures. A dated record — not a lightning bolt. What can a signature prove? What can it not?

### 3 · Motion pictures

Lab work was shared (including Dickson). One person peeping into a Kinetoscope is not the same design as a room watching a projected film. Scale changes the social experience, not only the machine.

> Is one-to-many viewing a different kind of innovation?

### 4 · Radio (counterexample)

Not an Edison invention claim. Many people; one public name.

> What vanishes when a crowded history becomes a story about one inventor?

### 5 · Motion Picture Patents Company

Patent pools, licenses, film stock — **market rules**, not one object. Shared standards can help production *and* lock others out.

> When does a shared standard become a gate?

## Money, companies, and the name

After the cases: investors, company formation, mergers. The famous **name** can stay on the poster while power moves to the firm.

**Optional watch:** [*A Day with Thomas A. Edison* (1922)](https://www.loc.gov/item/00694187/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener"} (Library of Congress). Who else appears on screen? How does a company film build the genius image?

Draw **two** maps — keep them separate:

1. **Who made it** — knowledge, experiments, skilled work, design  
2. **Who gets the value** — capital, rights, revenue, investment control  

The gap between the maps is the lesson.

## Vocabulary (use carefully)

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Invention | New principle or working device |
| Improvement | Better performance, use, or manufacture |
| Collaboration | Shared work — credited or not |
| Legal acquisition | Rights bought, licensed, or assigned by agreement |
| Appropriation | Taking credit, value, or control without recognition or agreement |
| Infringement | Court finding: used a protected invention without permission |
| Monopolization | Patents, pools, standards, or mergers used to lock others out |
| Patent capture | Legal rights used to control a wider market than the invention |
| Standard-setting | Shared rules that can open *or* choke access |
