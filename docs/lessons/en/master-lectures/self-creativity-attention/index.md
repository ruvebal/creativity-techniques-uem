## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish **open-monitoring** from **focused-attention** as different studio tools.
2. Connect OM to **divergent** work — and state the **limit** of FA for convergent work.
3. Plan an **attention brief** (when to open, when to focus) for a live design problem.
4. Run a short warm-up with **Opt-out** and dose honesty (~35 min study ≠ classroom minutes).

## Masterclass — six ideas

### 1 · Two jobs, two minds

Divergent work multiplies kinds; convergent work finds the fit. One creative act may hold both [(Craft 2000, 30)](#ref-craft-2000) — this course still **schedules them apart**.

### 2 · Open-monitoring vs focused attention

OM notices what arises without fixing; FA returns to one object. They are not the same practice [(Colzato, Ozturk, and Hommel 2012, 1)](#ref-colzato-2012).

### 3 · Opening feeds ideation

After open-monitoring, people did better on a divergent task in that laboratory study [(Colzato, Ozturk, and Hommel 2012, 1)](#ref-colzato-2012). Treat it as a useful bias — not a talent certificate.

### 4 · Focus is not magic selection

**Why:** Colzato et al. predicted that focused-attention training would help *convergent* thinking (finding the fit). That prediction did **not** hold in their results [(Colzato, Ozturk, and Hommel 2012, 1)](#ref-colzato-2012).

**What then:** Use FA when you already have options and need to *return to one object* — not as a spell that invents the right pick. Convergent work still needs criteria, comparison, and craft. Teach the limit so nobody leaves thinking “focus = automatic good selection.”

### 5 · Incubation needs partial attention

Walk, shower, craft — leave a problem half-held so new links can surface [(Csikszentmihalyi 1996)](#ref-csikszentmihalyi-1996).

### 6 · Dose honesty + Opt-out

**Opt-out** (defined here, repeated on the Lab card): you may **skip the embodied warm-up** — sit quietly or jump straight to listing — **without explaining why**. Consent before technique.

**Dose honesty:** the lab study used sessions of about **35 minutes**. Our classroom warm-up is shorter and **untested**. Adaptation is allowed; pretending the minutes match is not.

## Lab

### Exercise 1 — Open-monitoring warm-up

**Opt-out:** sit quietly or jump to listing — no explanation required. Then list idea **kinds** on today’s brief.

**Opt-in** means the default is non-participation: you take part only if you choose to. **Opt-out** means the default is participation: you take part unless you choose to skip.

### Exercise 2 — Attention brief

Two sentences: when you will **open**, when you will **focus**, for the live brief.
