> **For any system: who makes it, who pays, who owns it, who keeps it, who benefits?**

**Series:** Who Creates, Who Owns, Who Scales? · Lesson 2 of 2  
**Previous:** [Who Designs a Product—and Who Owns Its Story? (Edison)]({{ '/lessons/en/master-lectures/who-creates-edison/' | relative_url }})  
**In-class slideshow:** [open deck]({{ '/master-lectures/who-creates-fuller/' | relative_url }})

Fuller is a **comparison**, not Edison’s moral opposite. “Thinking in systems” is not automatically good — test it with the same three levels.

</div>

## Learning objectives

By the end you can:

1. Turn Fuller words into **questions you can test** (and keep integrity ≠ tensegrity).
2. Tell a striking **prototype** from a real **production system**.
3. Judge mass housing with both technical and money-and-power questions.
4. Fill an Edison–Fuller matrix from **evidence**, not slogans.
5. Redesign a system: money, rules, maintenance, and one trade-off you cannot remove.
6. Move the method to one digital product — without mixing patents, copyright, licenses, brands, and platforms.

## Reconnect (bring your dossier)

Same three levels:

| Level | Question | Fuller cases |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object | What works? | House, car, structure |
| System | What makes it usable at scale? | Factory, transport, assembly, upkeep |
| Money and power | Who funds, owns, and benefits? | Makers, investors, owners, residents |

> For Edison, what mattered most — object, system, or ownership? Does housing change your answer?

## Vocabulary lab

Turn each word into a question you can test. No slogans as proof.

| Word | Ask | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Synergy | What does the whole do that the parts alone cannot? | Name the interaction — not a pile of parts. |
| Integrity | Do parts, aims, and duties hold together? | Technical fit **and** duty to users. |
| Tensegrity | How do tension and compression hold the structure? | **Not** another word for ethics. |
| Ephemeralization | Same service, fewer resources? | Count make, use, repair, dispose. |
| Comprehensive design | Which linked problems sit inside the brief? | What was left outside? |

## Housing as industry

Four questions:

1. What must be standard for mass production?
2. What is made in a factory, and what must happen on site?
3. How is it paid for so people can access it?
4. Who maintains and adapts it over time?

## Edison–Fuller matrix

Fill from evidence and dossiers — not from ready-made morals.

| Ask | Edison | Fuller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where is the main creative contribution? | From case evidence | From case evidence |
| Who does the less-visible work? | Name them | Name them |
| How is development paid for? | Investment / ownership | Proposed or real finance |
| What enables scale? | Infrastructure, manufacture, standards | Factory, logistics, assembly |
| What blocks adoption? | Legal, technical, commercial | Rules, money, practice, users |
| What does the public story leave out? | Reputation vs records | Vision vs implementation |

### Exercise 2 — A Dymaxion Without a Genius

Propose housing, mobility, or a shared service. Deliver:

- Sketch of the service or object  
- Infrastructure diagram  
- Authorship map  
- Finance and ownership model  
- Maintenance and repair plan  
- One trade-off you cannot remove  

**Rule:** no unnamed investor, unlimited subsidy, unpaid upkeep, or perfectly obedient user.

## Transfer · one digital product

> Pick one Apple or Microsoft product. Which parts of its history are invention, interface, license, acquisition, standard, or public branding?

Be specific. “Jobs copied” or “Microsoft stole software” is too big without technology, people, contracts, and evidence.

## Final assignment

**Brief:** Redesign the object, redistribute the power

One Edison product + one Fuller proposal. Produce:

1. Short technical and social history of each  
2. Authorship map for each  
3. Finance and ownership comparison  
4. Redesigned object or service  
5. What changes when ownership, upkeep, or access changes?

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Accurate history and good evidence | 25% |
| Clear split: contribution / ownership / credit | 20% |
| Systems view, including money and maintenance | 25% |
| Plausible redesign | 20% |
| Uncertainty and trade-offs named | 10% |
