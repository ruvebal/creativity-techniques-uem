# D2 · Transposition

**Creativity Techniques · free format · creative work + critical review**

## What transposition means

Steimberg uses *transposition* for a work or genre passing across a semiotic language or material support:

> “A transposition occurs when a genre or a particular textual product changes support or language.”
>
> — (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Medios y géneros”; translated from Spanish by the course)

The transfer involves an interpretive choice:

> “Each transposition involves a choice about resignifying the work.”
>
> — (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Libro y transposición”; translated from Spanish by the course)

So a transposition is more than retelling a plot or changing a look. A novel made into a film, an oral riddle made for television, or a story redrawn as a comic changes the means through which the work can make meaning. The new form may carry, omit, or re-accent elements of the source; what an audience can read changes with it. In one example, red panels in an otherwise black-and-white comic reframe the literary story’s colour and rhythm (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Libro y transposición”). Fidelity is not the only useful measure: explain what your choices make possible and what they leave behind.

For this assignment, cross at least one **semiotic language/code** or **material support**. A change of medium may accompany that passage; a change in style alone does not meet Steimberg’s narrower definition.

## Constraint as discovery (Munari)
{: #constraint-as-discovery-munari }

Steimberg names the *crossing*. Munari names why a **formal rule** is not a cage: method and constraint are how discovery becomes shareable. Your D2 constraint ([Imposing Constraints]({{ '/exercises/en/imposing-constraints/' | relative_url }}) / [Dogme 95]({{ '/exercises/en/follow-dogme-95-rules/' | relative_url }})) should work the same way — remove options so a new reading can land.

## Course techniques for this process
{: #course-techniques-for-this-process }

D2 is not a free-form “make something else.” Reuse techniques already practised in this course. Name which ones you used in the process trail and the critical note.

**Core kit (use these):**

| Stage | Technique | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read the source | [One-inch picture frame]({{ '/exercises/en/one-inch-picture-frame/' | relative_url }}) | Narrow *what* can cross: one moment or element, not the whole source. |
| Prior practice | [U1 Lab · cut-up and readymade]({{ '/lessons/en/creativity-techniques/u-1-introduction-creativity/#lab-exercise-2' | relative_url }}) | Rehearsal of language / medium / support: change title, place, or carrier and ask what others see. |
| Choose the crossing | [Imposing Constraints]({{ '/exercises/en/imposing-constraints/' | relative_url }}) or [Follow Dogme 95 Rules]({{ '/exercises/en/follow-dogme-95-rules/' | relative_url }}) | One strict formal rule (palette, support ban, diegetic type only, no generative fill…). This is your Munari constraint. |
| Make and learn | [Question-led rough prototype]({{ '/exercises/en/question-led-rough-prototype/' | relative_url }}) | Smallest version that answers: *does this new language or support change the reading?* |
| During making | [Reflection-in-action log]({{ '/exercises/en/reflection-in-action-log/' | relative_url }}) | Log surprises and one changed move for the critical note. |
| Recognition gap | Adapt [Visitor Journey Walkthrough]({{ '/exercises/en/visitor-journey-walkthrough/' | relative_url }}) or [Hits and dot voting]({{ '/exercises/en/hits-and-dot-voting/' | relative_url }}) | Peer as audience: record what they notice first, or silent hits on what meaning lands — then compare to your intended reading. |
| Submit spine | [Process trail]({{ '/exercises/en/process-trail/' | relative_url }}) | Ordered versions, discarded option, diverge/converge marks, turning point, tool prompts. |

**When stuck (optional):**

- Only faithful remakes appear → [Random word stimulation]({{ '/exercises/en/random-word-stimulation/' | relative_url }}) or [Ask a Crab]({{ '/exercises/en/ask-a-crab/' | relative_url }}): force a lateral resignification path.
- “I must keep everything from the source” → [Mental locks audit]({{ '/exercises/en/mental-locks-audit/' | relative_url }}): name the fidelity lock; reopen one omitted path for five minutes.
- Source choice is vague → [Seed collection]({{ '/exercises/en/seed-collection/' | relative_url }}): gather candidates without ranking, then water three.

## The assignment

Choose one source artefact—your own or by someone else—and transpose it across a semiotic language/code or material support. Decide what to carry, omit, or change. Set one formal rule before you polish. You may also move genre, style, medium, or context. The new work should invite a reading; it need not reproduce the source’s original meaning.

Prototype early enough to learn from it. Keep a short process log. Show the work to a peer and compare the reading you intended with the reading you get. That gap is part of the work, not a failure.

Use the [course techniques kit](#course-techniques-for-this-process) above when you need a named method; name what you used in the process trail.

## Submit

Upload one package to Campus Virtual in the format and by the date announced there:

- **The transposed work:** any accessible format; a series is welcome.
- **A process trail:** follow [Process trail]({{ '/exercises/en/process-trail/' | relative_url }}) — the source and brief, your chosen constraint (from [Imposing Constraints]({{ '/exercises/en/imposing-constraints/' | relative_url }}) / [Dogme 95]({{ '/exercises/en/follow-dogme-95-rules/' | relative_url }})), at least one early or discarded version, diverge/converge marks, a turning-point note, and which [course techniques](#course-techniques-for-this-process) you used at which stage.
- **A short critical note:** 250–350 words, or a 2–3 minute audio/video reflection with captions or transcript.
- **Source and authorship note:** credit the source and collaborators; state any generative or assistive tools used. “No AI tools used” is a valid declaration.

In the critical note, briefly explain:

1. Using **Steimberg’s transposition** lens, how does the passage across language/code or support change what the source can mean, and what did you carry across? (Name the framing move — e.g. [one-inch picture frame]({{ '/exercises/en/one-inch-picture-frame/' | relative_url }}) — if you used it.)
2. Where does the work act: **language** (code), **medium** (channel or institution), and **support** (material carrier)? Name the relevant conditions of making and reception. (Reuse the U1 triad habit from [cut-up and readymade]({{ '/lessons/en/creativity-techniques/u-1-introduction-creativity/#lab-exercise-2' | relative_url }}).)
3. What reading did you expect, what reading did you encounter or anticipate, and where is the **gap** between them? (Cite peer evidence from your adapted [Visitor Journey]({{ '/exercises/en/visitor-journey-walkthrough/' | relative_url }}) or [Hits]({{ '/exercises/en/hits-and-dot-voting/' | relative_url }}) pass.)
4. Using **moral imagination** (Werhane & Bevan), who may benefit, be excluded, or be affected? Whose values, labour, or tool contributions are visible or hidden?
5. How did your chosen constraint shape the work? What did it help you discover ([Munari](#constraint-as-discovery-munari))? Tie the answer to [Imposing Constraints]({{ '/exercises/en/imposing-constraints/' | relative_url }}) or [Dogme 95]({{ '/exercises/en/follow-dogme-95-rules/' | relative_url }}), and quote or paraphrase one Munari claim from this page.
6. **Which course techniques did you use**, at which stage of the [map](#map-the-movement)? Name them from the [kit](#course-techniques-for-this-process); if you used an optional “when stuck” technique, say why.

Credit and use material you made, have permission to use, or can lawfully adapt. Make the work and reflection legible to your audience; add alt text or equivalent descriptions where needed.

## Rubric

This 0–10 rubric evaluates D2 within the official **40% delivery and/or presentation of work** category; it does not change the official course weighting.

| Criterion | Weight | 9–10 · Excellent | 7–8.9 · Competent | 5–6.9 · Minimum pass | 0–4.9 · Insufficient |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Transposition and creative judgement** | 25% | A purposeful passage across language/code or support makes a fresh reading possible; choices are precise and consequential. | The crossing is clear and the transformation is mostly purposeful. | A crossing is present, but its effect is slight or weakly explained. | Only surface style changes within the same language/support, the source is copied, or choices are unclear. |
| **Process, constraint, and revision** | 20% | Traces show exploration, a meaningful constraint, named course techniques at key stages, and a reasoned revision or refusal. | Process, constraint, and at least one course technique are visible; decisions are mostly explained. | Some process evidence, but choices, techniques, or revision are thinly explained. | Little process evidence; no clear constraint, course technique, or account of decisions. |
| **Circulation analysis** | 25% | Accurately locates language/medium/support and uses audience evidence to explain a specific production–recognition gap. | Names the triad and explains a plausible gap and audience reading. | Names some terms, but the gap or reading is general or unsupported. | Omits the triad or treats polish as proof that meaning was received. |
| **Ethical judgement and authorship** | 20% | Considers affected people and consequences; source, collaborators, and tool contributions are clearly credited. | Gives a relevant ethical consideration and complete credits/declaration. | Ethical reflection or attribution is limited; declaration is partial. | No meaningful ethical consideration, source credit, or authorship declaration. |
| **Communication and access** | 10% | Work and note are coherent, accessible, and well matched to the intended audience. | Work and note are clear and usable. | Some parts are difficult to follow or access. | Presentation prevents the work or reflection from being understood. |

## Keep in mind

Treat the source as a form shaped by genre, style, context, and audience. Ask what the new version does in circulation, whose reading it invites, and what responsibilities follow from your design choices. Techniques are tools, not scripts: the [kit](#course-techniques-for-this-process) moves the work; your judgement decides what to keep.
