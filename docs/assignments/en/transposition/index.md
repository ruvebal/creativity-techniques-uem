# D2 · Transposition

**Creativity Techniques · free format · creative work + critical review**

## What transposition means

Steimberg uses *transposition* for a work or genre passing across a semiotic language or material support:

> “A transposition occurs when a genre or a particular textual product changes support or language.”
>
> — (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Medios y géneros”; translated from Spanish by the course)

The transfer involves an interpretive choice:

> “Each transposition involves a choice about resignifying the work.”
>
> — (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Libro y transposición”; translated from Spanish by the course)

So a transposition is more than retelling a plot or changing a look. A novel made into a film, an oral riddle made for television, or a story redrawn as a comic changes the means through which the work can make meaning. The new form may carry, omit, or re-accent elements of the source; what an audience can read changes with it. In one example, red panels in an otherwise black-and-white comic reframe the literary story’s colour and rhythm (Steimberg 2013, sec. “Libro y transposición”). Fidelity is not the only useful measure: explain what your choices make possible and what they leave behind.

For this assignment, cross at least one **semiotic language/code** or **material support**. A change of medium may accompany that passage; a change in style alone does not meet Steimberg’s narrower definition.

## The assignment

Choose one source artefact—your own or by someone else—and transpose it across a semiotic language/code or material support. Make a deliberate choice about what to carry across, omit, or change. You may also move into a different genre, style, medium, or context. Your new work should invite a reading; it does not have to reproduce the source’s original meaning.

Put the work in front of a peer or a plausible audience. Compare the reading you intended with the reading you received or anticipate. Meaning can shift between making and recognition: that gap is part of the work, not proof that it failed.

## Submit

Upload one package to Campus Virtual in the format and by the date announced there:

- **The transposed work:** any accessible format; a series is welcome.
- **A process trail:** the source and brief, your chosen constraint, at least one early or discarded version, and a revision or decision.
- **A short critical note:** 250–350 words, or a 2–3 minute audio/video reflection with captions or transcript.
- **Source and authorship note:** credit the source and collaborators; state any generative or assistive tools used. “No AI tools used” is a valid declaration.

In the critical note, briefly explain:

1. Using **Steimberg’s transposition** lens, how does the passage across language/code or support change what the source can mean, and what did you carry across?
2. Where does the work act: **language** (code), **medium** (channel or institution), and **support** (material carrier)? Name the relevant conditions of making and reception.
3. What reading did you expect, what reading did you encounter or anticipate, and where is the **gap** between them?
4. Using **moral imagination** (Werhane & Bevan), who may benefit, be excluded, or be affected? Whose values, labour, or tool contributions are visible or hidden?
5. How did your chosen constraint shape the work? What did it help you discover (Munari)?

Credit and use material you made, have permission to use, or can lawfully adapt. Make the work and reflection legible to your audience; add alt text or equivalent descriptions where needed.

## Keep in mind

Treat the source as a form shaped by genre, style, context, and audience. Ask what the new version does in circulation, whose reading it invites, and what responsibilities follow from your design choices.
