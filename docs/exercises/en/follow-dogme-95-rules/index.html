Follow Dogme 95 Rules

Set one temporary formal rule for a short piece, make under that rule, then compare a freer pass. Built for Year-3 designers who already have craft — including treating “no generative fill” as one possible rule among others.

  • ~25 min
  • 1–2
  • technique constraint-writing
  • unit U5
  • unit U6

Source book Rubin, Rick. 2023. The Creative Act: A Way of Being. New York: Penguin Press.

On this page

  • Requirements
  • Steps

Requirements

Time ~25 minutes
Group 1–2
Access Rules can be oral, typed, or icon-based. A seated version uses the same rule on a small digital canvas. Voice notes count as the freer comparison pass.

Materials

  • one shared brief or current D1 problem
  • paper, layout tool, or other medium you already use
  • optional generative tool only if your chosen rule allows it

Before you start

  • You already have a short piece to make (text, layout, sound, image sequence).
  • Agree the rule before you open any generative tool.
  • Do not photograph people; this exercise is about formal rules, not fieldwork.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Choose a short piece tied to your brief (about fifteen minutes of making).
  2. Write one strict temporary rule. Examples adapted for design studio — only five shapes; one colour; diegetic type only (words that could exist in the scene); no generative fill; one language only.
  3. Make the piece under the rule within the time limit.
  4. Make a second version without the rule (or with the rule relaxed).
  5. Compare which version is more surprising, and which better fits the brief. Name one craft move the rule forced.

Source

The imposition of rules is most valuable for an artist who has already made some work. If you’re established in a craft or field, temporary rules may be useful to break a pattern. They can challenge you to become better, to innovate, and to bring out a new side of yourself or your work. […] The director Lars von Trier came up with ten rules, Dogme 95 The Vow of Chastity, designed to reduce the artificiality of filmmaking. They were as follows: Shooting must be performed on location, without providing props or sets that don’t logically exist within that setting. Diegetic sound only. Sounds must never be produced, such as music that does not exist within the scene. All shots must be handheld. Movement, immobility, and stability must be attained by hand. […] The rules of baseball or basketball define the game and are rarely altered. Innovation exists only within those rules. As artists, we get to create a new set of rules each and every time we play. After careful consideration, we may choose to break them in the midst of a project if a discovery impels us. While it’s easy to make these changes, there’s little use to rules if they are not taken seriously.

— (Rubin 2023, 65)

Classroom note. Classroom timing (25 minutes), design-studio rule examples, and optional GenAI as a rule among others are professor adaptations of Rubin’s Temporary Rules / Dogme 95 discussion. Steps follow the course canonical technique constraint-writing.

Portfolio

The rule in one line; both versions (or photos of them); one sentence on what the rule forced; AI declaration if a generative tool was used.

Judged by: Portfolio rubric — creative fluency, flexibility, and originality
