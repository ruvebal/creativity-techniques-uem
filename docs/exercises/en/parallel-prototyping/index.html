Parallel Prototyping

Make three quick prototypes side by side before anyone gives feedback, then two more, then a final. In the source study, designers who worked this way produced better and more varied results, and felt more confident, than designers who refined one prototype at a time.

  • ~70 min
  • individual makers, with a peer giving feedback
  • technique parallel-prototyping
  • unit U3
  • unit U5

Source book Dow, Steven P., Alana Glassco, Jonathan Kass, Melissa Schwarz, Daniel L. Schwartz, and Scott R. Klemmer. 2010. “Parallel Prototyping Leads to Better Design Results, More Divergence, and Increased Self-Efficacy.” ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction 17 (4): article 18. Requirements

Time ~70 minutes
Group individual makers, with a peer giving feedback
Access Any medium is valid (paper, slides, Figma, cardboard). Feedback may be written on cards instead of spoken. The session can be split across two classes: three prototypes and feedback in one, the rest in the next.

Materials

  • one short brief (poster, banner, screen, label, or similar)
  • paper or a simple design tool
  • timer
  • feedback cards (one line of description per prototype, no ranking)

Before you start

  • Prototypes must be rough — about ten minutes each.
  • Feedback describes what a prototype does; it does not say which one is best.
  • Keep every prototype, numbered in the order made.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Read the brief. Set a timer and make three different prototypes, about ten minutes each. Do not stop to polish.
  2. Swap with a peer. They write one descriptive feedback line on each of the three (about five minutes).
  3. Make two more prototypes (ten minutes each), using what you learned from all three.
  4. Get one feedback line on each of the two new ones.
  5. Make a final version (fifteen minutes). It may combine parts of any earlier prototype.
  6. Lay all six side by side. Write which earlier prototype the final borrows from, and what you would have missed with only one.

Source

The experiment employed a between-subjects design with one independent variable: the structure of the prototyping process. The study held constant the number of prototypes created, the amount of feedback provided, and the overall time allotted. In the parallel condition, participants created 3 prototypes and got feedback, then made 2 more and got more feedback, and then made a final version. In the serial condition, participants created 5 prototypes in a series, received feedback after each prototype, then made a final version. Parallel participants were instructed to start subsequent prototypes at the same intervals as serial participants.

— (Dow et al. 2010, 18:5)

Classroom note. Dow and colleagues ran this structure as a controlled experiment with web banner ads and an expert's scripted critique. This classroom version keeps their timings and the 3 → feedback → 2 → feedback → final structure; peer feedback cards replace the expert critique, and the side-by-side reflection is a professor addition.

Portfolio

Six numbered prototypes (photos); the five feedback lines; one paragraph tracing what the final borrows.
