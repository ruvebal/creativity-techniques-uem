Alternative Uses Task

List many uses for one ordinary object under a timer. Fluency first; then notice flexibility across categories.

~15 min · individual, then shared board · U1, U2

Source book Chen, Chaomei. 2011. Turning Points: The Nature of Creativity. Beijing: Higher Education Press; Berlin: Springer.

Brainstorm Ground Rules

Run a short group idea session under the four original brainstorming rules: no criticism, wild ideas welcome, go for quantity, build on others. A rule-keeper calls out any judgement, and selection happens only after the session ends.

~30 min · 4–6, with one scribe and one rule-keeper · U2

Source book Osborn, Alex F. 2013. Applied Imagination: Principles and Procedures of Creative Problem-Solving. Read Books.

Used in U2

  • technique brainstorming-osborn

Consider the Negative

List the unwritten rules of a familiar style or product type, then negate one deep rule — do its opposite — and sketch what becomes possible. Changing a structural rule, not a surface detail, can open a whole new space of designs.

~30 min · pairs · U5, U6

Source book Boden, Margaret A. 2004. The Creative Mind: Myths and Mechanisms. 2nd ed. London: Routledge.

Used in Master lecture, U5, U6.2

WIP

Down Draft, Up Draft

Make the same piece three times with three different jobs: get it down, fix it up, then check every detail. Separating the passes stops you from judging work before it exists.

~40 min · individual, with one peer check · U3, U5

Source book Lamott, Anne. 2007. Bird by Bird: Some Instructions on Writing and Life. New York: Anchor Books.

Used in U3

Follow Dogme 95 Rules

Set one temporary formal rule for a short piece, make under that rule, then compare a freer pass. Built for Year-3 designers who already have craft — including treating “no generative fill” as one possible rule among others.

~25 min · 1–2 · U5, U6

Source book Rubin, Rick. 2023. The Creative Act: A Way of Being. New York: Penguin Press.

Hits and dot voting

WIP

Idea-Spurring Questions

Take one existing object, layout, or service and run it through Osborn's nine questions: other uses, adapt, modify, magnify, minify, substitute, rearrange, reverse, combine. One quick sketch per question gives nine variants to choose from. (This checklist is the source of the later SCAMPER acronym.)

~35 min · individual or pairs · U2, U4

Source book Osborn, Alex F. 2013. Applied Imagination: Principles and Procedures of Creative Problem-Solving. Read Books.

  • unit U2

Morning Pages

Write three pages of fast, unedited longhand before you start a design task. The pages are private and never assessed; their job is to empty out the inner critic so the next idea pass starts with a clearer head.

WIP

Parallel Prototyping

Make three quick prototypes side by side before anyone gives feedback, then two more, then a final. In the source study, designers who worked this way produced better and more varied results, and felt more confident, than designers who refined one prototype at a time.

~70 min · individual makers, with a peer giving feedback · U3, U5

Source book Dow, Steven P., Alana Glassco, Jonathan Kass, Melissa Schwarz, Daniel L. Schwartz, and Scott R. Klemmer. 2010. “Parallel Prototyping Leads to Better Design Results, More Divergence, and Increased Self-Efficacy.” ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction 17 (4): article 18. [https://doi.org/10.1145/1879831.1879836](https://doi.org/10.1145/1879831.1879836).

Pure Contour Drawing

Draw the creases of your own palm for five minutes without looking at the paper. The drawing will not look like a hand; that is the point. It trains slow, exact looking and switches off the habit of drawing what you think things look like.

~15 min · individual, then pairs to compare · U1, U4

Source book Edwards, Betty. 1999. The New Drawing on the Right Side of the Brain. New York: Jeremy P. Tarcher/Putnam.

Yes, And

Build one idea as a group by answering every offer with "Yes, and…". Then run the same round with "Yes, but…" and compare what happened to the idea and to the group.

~25 min · 4–6 in a circle · U2, U4

Source book Nixon, Natalie. 2020. The Creativity Leap: Unleash Curiosity, Improvisation, and Intuition at Work. Oakland, CA: Berrett-Koehler.

Used in U2, U4.1
