Alternative Uses Task

List many uses for one ordinary object under a timer. Fluency first; then notice flexibility across categories.

~15 min · individual, then shared board · U1, U2

Source book Chen, Chaomei. 2011. Turning Points: The Nature of Creativity. Beijing: Higher Education Press; Berlin: Springer.

  • unit U1

Ask a Crab — picture diverge

Use an unrelated picture to open workplace ideas before you judge. Save the path from image to idea — not only the favourite.

~20 min · same team as the U4 idea set · U4

Source book Michalko, Michael. 2010. Thinkertoys: A Handbook of Creative-Thinking Techniques. 2nd ed. Berkeley: Ten Speed Press.

  • technique brainstorming-osborn
  • unit U4

ready

Follow Dogme 95 Rules

Set one temporary formal rule for a short piece, make under that rule, then compare a freer pass. Built for Year-3 designers who already have craft — including treating “no generative fill” as one possible rule among others.

~25 min · 1–2 · U5, U6

Source book Rubin, Rick. 2023. The Creative Act: A Way of Being. New York: Penguin Press.

  • technique constraint-writing
  • unit U5
  • unit U6

ready

Hits and dot voting

Mark interesting parts in silence before you debate. Hits gather heat; a later vote decides — without groupthink in the first pass.

~15 min · 3–10 · U2, U4

Source book Knapp, Jake, John Zeratsky, and Braden Kowitz. 2016. Sprint: How to Solve Big Problems and Test New Ideas in Just Five Days. New York: Simon & Schuster.

  • technique hits-dot-voting
  • unit U2
  • unit U4

ready

Imposing Constraints

Add a deliberate constraint to a freewheeling idea pass so craft pressure produces form. Same technique family as Follow Dogme 95 Rules, with a philosophical warrant that constraints can enhance creativity.

~25 min · 1–2 · U5, U6

Source book Kieran, Matthew. 2018. In Creativity and Philosophy, edited by Berys Gaut and Matthew Kieran. New York: Routledge.

  • technique constraint-writing
  • unit U5
  • unit U6

Mental locks audit

Name the belief that closed an idea. Reopen one locked path for five minutes — personal development as craft, not soft-skill theatre.

~15 min · individual · U6

One-inch picture frame

Narrow a broad design brief to one small, observable moment. Make a quick sketch or description of only what fits inside that frame, then use the missing details to decide what to explore next.

~20 min · individual, then pair review · U3

Source book Lamott, Anne. 2007. Bird by Bird: Some Instructions on Writing and Life. New York: Anchor Books.

  • technique entry-point-attention-area
  • unit U3

Process trail

~20 min · individual; optional pair read · U1, U5

Question-led rough prototype

Build the smallest surface that answers one test question. Optional generative tools stay in early variation; you keep the final form.

~20 min · alone to make; one classmate tests · U3, U5

Random word stimulation

Force a lateral jump: relate a random word to your brief, then show the path. Same word, many situations.

~10 min · individual, then brief share · U2, U5

Source book de Bono, Edward. 1970. Lateral Thinking: A Textbook of Creativity. London: Ward Lock Educational.

  • unit U2
  • unit U5

Reflection-in-action log

Log surprises while you work — when the situation talks back — then name one move you changed.

~15 min · individual · U3, U6

Source book Schön, Donald A. 1983. The Reflective Practitioner: How Professionals Think in Action. New York: Basic Books.

Seed collection

Collect many small starting points without ranking them. Later, water the ones that still resonate — photos, phrases, and sounds all count.

~20 min · individual or pairs · U2, U4, U6

Source book Rubin, Rick. 2023. The Creative Act: A Way of Being. New York: Penguin Press.

  • unit U2
  • unit U4

Visitor Journey Walkthrough

Walk a Creative Campus route from a first-time visitor’s side. Record actions and words; select one prior idea against evidence — not against taste.

~20 min · team; rotate walker, observer, recorder · U4

Source book Michalko, Michael. 2010. Thinkertoys: A Handbook of Creative-Thinking Techniques. 2nd ed. Berkeley: Ten Speed Press.
