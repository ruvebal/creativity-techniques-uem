Random word stimulation

Force a lateral jump: relate a random word to your brief, then show the path. Same word, many situations.

  • ~10 min
  • individual, then brief share
  • technique random-word
  • unit U2
  • unit U5

Source book de Bono, Edward. 1970. Lateral Thinking: A Textbook of Creativity. London: Ward Lock Educational.

On this page

  • Requirements

Requirements

Time ~10 minutes
Group individual, then brief share
Access Spoken paths accepted; seated work OK.

Materials

  • random word (card, book, or app)
  • brief
  • paper

Before you start

  • Agree one shared brief.
  • Do not swap the random word mid-pass.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. State the problem in one sentence.
  2. Draw or receive one random word.
  3. Write three links from the word to the problem without judging them.
  4. Pick one link and sketch or note a design move it suggests.
  5. Optional: one student describes their path to the class so others see the same word used differently.

Source

The students each write down any problem they would like to tackle. They write it down in duplicate, put a name or number on each sheet, and give one copy to the teacher. This is to prevent a sudden change in the problem when the random word is given. A random word is then found (by page number etc suggested by the students to locate a word in the dictionary or just chosen by the teacher). […] Before they hand in their results some of the students are asked by the teacher to describe to the rest of the class how they related the word to their own problem. In this type of session one can get an idea of how the same random word can be of use in many different situations. If some students find that they cannot make any progress at all then the teacher goes through the problem with them showing how the random word might be used in each case.

— (de Bono 1970, 28)

Classroom note. Ten-minute class clock and design-studio wording are professor adaptations of de Bono’s random-word classroom session.

Portfolio

Problem sentence; random word; three links; chosen move.

Judged by: Portfolio rubric — creative fluency, flexibility, and originality
