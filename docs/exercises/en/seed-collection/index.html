Seed collection

Collect many small starting points without ranking them. Later, water the ones that still resonate — photos, phrases, and sounds all count.

  • ~20 min
  • individual or pairs
  • technique seed-collection
  • unit U2
  • unit U4
  • unit U6

Source book Rubin, Rick. 2023. The Creative Act: A Way of Being. New York: Penguin Press.

Requirements

Time ~20 minutes
Group individual or pairs
Access Voice notes, photos or text all count as seeds.

Materials

  • notebook or phone notes
  • optional camera for tools/fixtures only (no people)

Before you start

  • Do not rank while collecting.
  • If photographing on campus: tools and fixtures only; no faces or badges.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Collect at least five seeds (phrase, sound, image, tool, fixture) in one place.
  2. Do not compare or rank them while collecting.
  3. Pick three seeds that still interest you.
  4. Develop each in a different direction for five minutes.
  5. Keep the seed that grew most; note why.

Source

Placing too much emphasis on a seed or dismissing it prematurely can interfere with its natural growth. The temptation to insert too much of yourself in this first phase can undermine the entire enterprise. Be wary of taking shortcuts or crossing items off your list too quickly. The seed that doesn’t get watered cannot reveal its ability to bear fruit. Collect many seeds and then, over time, look back and see which ones resonate. Sometimes we’re too close to them to recognize their true potential, and other times the magical moment that inspired a seed into existence is bigger than the seed itself. It’s generally preferable to accumulate several weeks’ or months’ worth of ideas and then choose which of them to focus on, instead of following an urge or obligation to rush to the finish line with what is in front of us today.

— (Rubin 2023, 50)

Classroom note. Compressed class timing; optional Creative Campus tool/fixture photography with a no-people rule is a professor adaptation.

Portfolio

Five seeds; three developed; one kept with reason; consent note if photos.

Judged by: Portfolio rubric — creative fluency, flexibility, and originality
