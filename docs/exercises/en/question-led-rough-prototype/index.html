Exercises · U3, U5

Question-led rough prototype

Build the smallest surface that answers one test question. Optional generative tools stay in early variation; you keep the final form.

  • ~20 min
  • alone to make; one classmate tests
  • technique question-led-prototype
  • unit U3
  • unit U5

Source book Knapp, Jake, John Zeratsky, and Braden Kowitz. 2016. Sprint: How to Solve Big Problems and Test New Ideas in Just Five Days. New York: Simon & Schuster.

Requirements

Time ~20 minutes
Group alone to make; one classmate tests
Access Seated digital mock-ups accepted; oral test answers OK.

Materials

  • shared class brief or D1 problem
  • paper/card or digital mock-up tool
  • optional generative tool for early variation only

Before you start

  • Write the test question before you build.
  • If you use GenAI, log prompt, keep, and re-author moves.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Write the one question this prototype must answer.
  2. Decide whether it tests role, look and feel, or how it works.
  3. Build only the surface needed to answer it — by hand, code, or staged tool output you then edit.
  4. If a generative tool supplied a variant, log the prompt, what you kept, and what you re-authored.
  5. Let one classmate try the prototype; record the answer to your question.

Source

Don’t prototype anything you aren’t willing to throw away. Remember: This solution might not work. So don’t give in to the temptation of spending a few days or weeks getting your prototype ready. You’ll have diminishing returns on that extra work, and all the while, you’ll be falling deeper in love with a solution that could turn out to be a loser. […] The prototype is meant to answer questions, so keep it focused. You don’t need a fully functional product—you just need a real-looking façade to which customers can react.

— (Knapp, Zeratsky, and Kowitz 2016, chap. 13)

Classroom note. 20-minute class timing; optional GenAI for variation only; mandatory prompt/edit log are professor adaptations of the sprint prototype mindset.
