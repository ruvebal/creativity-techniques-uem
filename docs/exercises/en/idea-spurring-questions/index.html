Idea-Spurring Questions

Take one existing object, layout, or service and run it through Osborn's nine questions: other uses, adapt, modify, magnify, minify, substitute, rearrange, reverse, combine. One quick sketch per question gives nine variants to choose from. (This checklist is the source of the later SCAMPER acronym.)

  • ~35 min
  • individual or pairs
  • technique scamper
  • unit U2
  • unit U4

Source book Osborn, Alex F. 2013. Applied Imagination: Principles and Procedures of Creative Problem-Solving. Read Books.

Requirements

Time ~35 minutes
Group individual or pairs
Access Answers can be drawn, written, typed, or spoken to a partner who sketches. The card can be read aloud. Pairs may split the nine questions between them.

Materials

  • one existing thing to change (product, poster, screen, room, service step)
  • a sheet folded into nine boxes, or a nine-frame digital board
  • the nine questions printed on a card
  • timer

Before you start

  • Start from something that already exists; the questions work on a concrete starting point, not a blank page.
  • One sketch or note per box; quantity before quality.
  • Any working language is fine; sketches count as answers.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Draw or paste the starting thing in the centre of the page. Write one line on what it is for.
  2. Set a timer for three minutes per question. Work through the nine questions in order.
  3. In each box, make one quick sketch or note that answers that question. If stuck, read the sub-questions on the card.
  4. When all nine boxes are full, star the two variants that most change how the thing works, not just how it looks.
  5. Develop one starred variant into a slightly larger sketch with a one-line reason.

Source

Put to other uses? New ways to use as is? Other uses if modified? Adapt? What else is like this? What other idea does this suggest? Does past offer parallel? What could I copy? Whom could I emulate? Modify? New twist? Change meaning, color, motion, sound, odor, form, shape? Other changes? Magnify? What to add? More time? Greater frequency? Stronger? Higher? Longer? Thicker? Extra value? Plus ingredient? Duplicate? Multiply? Exaggerate? Minify? What to subtract? Smaller? Condensed? Miniature? Lower? Shorter? Lighter? Omit? Streamline? Split up? Understate? Substitute? Who else instead? What else instead? Other ingredient? Other material? Other process? Other power? Other place? Other approach? Other tone of voice? Rearrange? Interchange components? Other pattern? Other layout? Other sequence? Transpose cause and effect? Change pace? Change schedule? Reverse? Transpose positive and negative? How about opposites? Turn it backward? Turn it upside down? Reverse roles? Change shoes? Turn tables? Turn other cheek? Combine? How about a blend, an alloy, an assortment, an ensemble? Combine units? Combine purposes? Combine appeals? Combine ideas?

— (Osborn 2013, chap. 24)

Classroom note. Osborn lists these questions as a self-questioning summary for finding alternatives. The nine-box sheet, three-minute timing, and the "changes how it works" selection step are professor adaptations. Later authors condensed the list into the SCAMPER acronym; this datasheet uses Osborn's own wording.
