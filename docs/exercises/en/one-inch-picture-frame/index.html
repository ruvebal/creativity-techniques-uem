One-inch picture frame

Narrow a broad design brief to one small, observable moment. Make a quick sketch or description of only what fits inside that frame, then use the missing details to decide what to explore next.

  • ~20 min
  • individual, then pair review
  • technique entry-point-attention-area
  • unit U3

Source book Lamott, Anne. 2007. Bird by Bird: Some Instructions on Writing and Life. New York: Anchor Books.

On this page

  • Requirements
  • Steps
  • Source
  • Portfolio

Requirements

Time ~20 minutes
Group individual, then pair review
Access Students may draw, write, type, dictate, or use a cropped digital image. No physical frame is required.

Materials

  • current design brief or project idea
  • paper and pen, or a small digital canvas
  • optional paper frame or crop tool

Before you start

  • Choose one user, object, or moment from the brief.
  • Keep this pass exploratory; do not grade the first sketch or description.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Write the broad brief at the top of the page.
  2. Choose one small moment, view, or interaction that can be described in a single frame.
  3. Spend five minutes sketching or describing only what is visible in that frame; mark unknowns as questions.
  4. Show the frame to a partner. Ask what they notice first and what remains unclear.
  5. Use one observation and one unanswered question to set the next design move.

Source

It reminds me that all I have to do is to write down as much as I can see through a one-inch picture frame. […] All I am going to do right now, for example, is write that one paragraph that sets the story in my hometown, in the late fifties, when the trains were still running. I am going to paint a picture of it, in words, on my word processor. Or all I am going to do is to describe the main character the very first time we meet her, when she first walks out the front door and onto the porch. […] just what I can see through the one-inch picture frame, just one paragraph describing this woman, in the town where I grew up, the first time we encounter her.

— (Lamott 2007, 11)

Classroom note. Lamott describes narrowing a writing task to one small picture frame and one paragraph. This classroom adaptation transfers that scale of attention to a design brief; the five-minute timing and peer prompt are instructor choices.

Portfolio

One-frame sketch or description; the observation from the partner; the unanswered question; the next move it suggests.

Judged by: Portfolio rubric — problem framing, purposeful observation, and reasoned development
