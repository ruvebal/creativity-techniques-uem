Requirements

Time ~15 minutes
Group individual
Access Typed or oral audit OK.

Materials

  • a dropped or blocked idea from recent work
  • paper

Before you start

  • Stay tied to a making brief.
  • Do not treat this as therapy.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Pick one idea you dropped this week.
  2. Write the belief or rule that closed it (the “lock”).
  3. Reopen the idea for five minutes under a temporary opposite rule.
  4. Note what appeared that the lock had blocked.
  5. Decide keep, transform, or shelve — with one craft reason.

Source

Mental locks are the beliefs that close an idea before it has been tested—“that is not logical,” “we already tried that,” “people like us don’t do this.” The classroom audit asks you to name one lock that ended a recent idea, ask when that belief helps and when it blocks, then reopen the dropped idea for five private minutes with the lock set aside. What changes in those five minutes is the evidence; the lock itself is not graded as a confession. (Classroom adaptation of Open Mental Locks catalogue records; von Oech is held, not cited as a student quotation here.)
