Visitor Journey Walkthrough

Walk a Creative Campus route from a first-time visitor’s side. Record actions and words; select one prior idea against evidence — not against taste.

  • ~20 min
  • team; rotate walker, observer, recorder
  • technique contextual-observation
  • unit U4

Source book Michalko, Michael. 2010. Thinkertoys: A Handbook of Creative-Thinking Techniques. 2nd ed. Berkeley: Ten Speed Press.

On this page

Requirements

Time ~20 minutes
Group team; rotate walker, observer, recorder
Access Seated route-map walkthrough allowed; mark observed vs imagined parts.

Materials

  • prior idea set (e.g. U4.1)
  • agreed public campus route to the Atrium information point
  • route note sheet

Before you start

  • Do not impersonate a disability or claim to represent a community.
  • Do not record identifiable members of the public.
  • Peer walkthroughs are not visitor research.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Agree the Creative Campus route, destination, and a neutral first-time visitor task.
  2. One teammate walks without seeing the idea set; the observer records actions, words, pauses, and turns.
  3. Switch roles or use a seated map walkthrough if needed.
  4. Compare notes with the idea set; select one idea that responds to a recorded moment; name the criterion and one unresolved question.

Source

Walking in the customer's shoes. In this approach, people turn around to find out what it's really like to be someone in a different position. For example, a realtor sent his salespeople to car dealerships to help them improve their selling skills. Posing as customers, they walked through the entire sales process, recording particular behavior, words, and actions that critically affected their attitude as 'buyers.' At the sales meeting, they shared their experiences and talked about ways they could improve their own selling skills. It was an eye-opening experience that made the salespeople see that it's one thing to talk about walking in someone else's shoes—and another to actually do it.

— (Michalko 2010, 371)

Classroom note. Professor adaptation of “Walking in the Customer’s Shoes” to a short Creative Campus route with portfolio fields.

Portfolio

Route and task; roles; observation notes; selected idea and criterion; unresolved question; limitation note.
