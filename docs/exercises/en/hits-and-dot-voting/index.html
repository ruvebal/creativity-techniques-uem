Hits and dot voting

Mark interesting parts in silence before you debate. Hits gather heat; a later vote decides — without groupthink in the first pass.

  • ~15 min
  • 3–10
  • technique hits-dot-voting
  • unit U2
  • unit U4

Source book Knapp, Jake, John Zeratsky, and Braden Kowitz. 2016. Sprint: How to Solve Big Problems and Test New Ideas in Just Five Days. New York: Simon & Schuster.

On this page

Requirements

Time ~15 minutes
Group 3–10
Access Digital whiteboard voting; allow seated access to the wall.

Materials

  • idea sheets on a wall or board
  • dot stickers
  • optional digital whiteboard

Before you start

  • Ideas must already be visible (one per sheet).
  • No talking during the heat-map pass.

Steps

Follow in order — your professor may shorten the clock.

  1. Put all ideas on the wall, one per note or sheet.
  2. Read in silence; each person places small dots on parts that look strong (hits).
  3. Each person then gets one larger vote for one idea.
  4. Cluster the dots and read out the top three.
  5. The decider (or the group) chooses, and records why.

Source

We’ve spent years optimizing our sprint decisions to be as efficient as possible. We ended up with a five-step process—and coincidentally, every step involves something sticky: 1. Art museum: Put the solution sketches on the wall with masking tape. 2. Heat map: Look at all the solutions in silence, and use dot stickers to mark interesting parts. 3. Speed critique: Quickly discuss the highlights of each solution, and use sticky notes to capture big ideas. 4. Straw poll: Each person chooses one solution, and votes for it with a dot sticker. 5. Supervote: The Decider makes the final decision, with—you guessed it—more stickers. This sticky stuff isn’t a gimmick. The dot stickers let us form and express our opinions without lengthy debate, and the sticky notes allow us to record big ideas without relying on our short-term memory.

— (Knapp, Zeratsky, and Kowitz 2016, chap. 10)
