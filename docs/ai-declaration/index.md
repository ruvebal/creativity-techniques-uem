# AI Usage Declaration

**Creativity Techniques · authorship and transparency guidelines**

- Table of Contents
  {:toc}

## Purpose

This declaration supports transparency about the use of artificial intelligence tools in course work. It does not prohibit AI; it requires clear documentation of what tools were used, how they were used, and what human decisions shaped the final work.

## Required declaration elements

Every submitted piece (portfolio entry, exercise, project, or exam response) must include:

- **Tools used:** name specific AI tools (e.g., ChatGPT, Midjourney, Claude, GitHub Copilot, etc.) or state "No AI tools used"
- **Purpose of use:** describe what the AI was asked to do (e.g., "generate initial ideas," "check grammar," "suggest code structure," "create reference images")
- **Human contribution:** explain what you decided, modified, rejected, or added after AI interaction
- **Source material:** if the AI was trained on or prompted with specific references, name them

## Example declarations

### Example 1: No AI used

> **AI tools used:** None  
> **Human contribution:** All ideation, sketching, selection, and revision were done manually.

### Example 2: AI for ideation support

> **AI tools used:** ChatGPT (GPT-4)  
> **Purpose of use:** Generated 20 initial concepts for the sustainability brief to expand beyond my first 3 ideas.  
> **Human contribution:** I selected concept #7, adapted it to the course constraints, developed the visual sketches myself, and revised the concept based on peer feedback. The AI provided raw material; I shaped it into the final proposal.

### Example 3: AI for technical assistance

> **AI tools used:** GitHub Copilot  
> **Purpose of use:** Suggested JavaScript syntax for the portfolio navigation menu.  
> **Human contribution:** I reviewed every suggestion, kept only what matched my intended structure, rewrote unclear parts, and tested the final code. The AI accelerated typing but did not design the interaction logic.

### Example 4: AI for visual reference

> **AI tools used:** Midjourney v6  
> **Purpose of use:** Created moodboard images for colour and texture exploration.  
> **Human contribution:** I used the AI images as reference only, did not copy them directly, and created all final artwork in Photoshop. The AI helped me see colour combinations I had not considered.

## Principles

1. **Transparency over prohibition:** The course does not ban AI tools; it requires that their use be documented.
2. **Human authorship remains central:** AI can assist, but the creative decisions, critical judgement, and final responsibility are yours.
3. **Context matters:** Using AI to generate an entire submission without human contribution is not acceptable practice. Using AI as one tool among many (like a reference book, a tutorial, or a peer conversation) is acceptable when documented.
4. **Honesty about uncertainty:** If you are unsure whether a tool counts as AI, declare it. The emphasis is on clarity, not on technical classification.

## What this declaration does not replace

This AI declaration does not replace:

- **Academic integrity rules:** plagiarism, misrepresentation, or dishonesty remain violations regardless of tool use
- **Source citations:** traditional references (books, articles, websites) must still be cited properly
- **Collaboration credit:** if you worked with others, name them and describe their contributions
- **Material permissions:** ensure you have rights to use any source material, whether human- or AI-generated

## Questions

If you are uncertain about how to declare AI use in a specific situation, ask during class or office hours. The goal is to develop habits of transparent authorship that will serve you in professional practice.

## Scholarly grounding

Research on AI in design education supports transparency as essential for maintaining academic integrity and student authorship. Bartlett and Camba (2024) emphasize that AI should be a tool, not the creator, and recommend documenting AI use and decisions for legal and educational reasons. Embedding principles of transparency in AI use helps preserve fairness while fostering ethical and responsible use (Anwar et al. 2025). Ethical evaluation of AI-assisted work for cultural appropriateness, attribution, and data transparency aligns with UNESCO's call for responsible, human-centered AI use in education (Anwar et al. 2025).

The EU AI Act (2024) provides a regulatory framework that aligns with these educational principles, requiring transparency in AI models to ensure copyright compliance and mandating that deployers disclose when AI has been used in content creation (European Parliament & Council of the European Union, 2024). This legal framework recognizes both the development opportunities offered by generative models and the need for ethical integration of AI in creative domains.
