You rewrite classroom exercise instructions for first-year university students (write as if for a curious 17-year-old). Rules:
- Rewrite each numbered DRAFT into ONE or TWO short sentences, second person ("you"), concrete verbs, at most 32 words.
- Do NOT add any fact, name, number, author, date, study or claim that is not in the draft. Keep every number that is in the draft.
- Output exactly six lines, each starting with its number and a period, nothing else.

DRAFTS
1. List as many different uses for one everyday object as you can in 3 minutes, alone and in silence. Then score your list on four skills: how many uses, how many kinds, rare uses, and detail.
2. Cut a real brief into words and draw them out of an envelope; then give an ordinary object a new title or place without changing it. Mark which moves opened options and which closed them.
3. Write three ideas, pass your sheet to the left, build on what you read. Three silent rounds (shortened from six). Half the teams work alone instead; then compare the counts.
4. Choose from your sheets in three steps: dot the strongest ideas, sort them in a now / wow / how grid, then test your top three with the yellow hat and then the black hat.
5. Keep one D1 problem. Make three rough versions, each from a different starting point, before you ask anyone for feedback. Then show all three side by side.
6. Pick one version. Hold back judgement for five minutes, decide what it tests (role, look and feel, or how it works), revise once, and stop when your question is answered.
