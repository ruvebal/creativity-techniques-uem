STEM: What is the system of three parts that Csikszentmihalyi describes for creativity?
ANSWER: Domain, Field, and Person.
DISTRACTORS: 
EVIDENCE: Csikszentmihalyi calls that assumption misleading: for him, creativity can be observed only in a system of three parts — a domain (a set of rules and symbols, such as graphic design), a field (the people who decide what gets in: teachers, clients, curators, critics), and the person who brings something new [(Csikszentmihalyi 1996, chap. 2)].

ANSWER: This process involves divergent thinking (opening) followed by convergent thinking (closing).

EVIDENCE: Opening is divergent thinking: you invent many possibilities. Closing is convergent thinking: you select what fits the needs of the brief.
